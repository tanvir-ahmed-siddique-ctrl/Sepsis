**SECTION 01**

VISION & STRATEGIC INTENT

The International AI Builders Congress 2026 is not a conference, and it is not a hackathon. It is a six-month builder journey designed to transform participants into world-class AI product builders capable of creating globally scalable products, venture-backed startups, intellectual property, jobs, and measurable economic value.

From July through December, participants learn, build, collaborate, mentor, validate, and launch solutions with the potential to become globally competitive businesses. The journey culminates on December 26, 2026, when more than 5,000 builders gather at Daffodil International University to showcase production-ready innovations, compete, and connect with investors and employers.

The Congress exists to answer one question: how do we develop the next generation of builders who can create products that compete globally and solve meaningful problems? It brings universities, industry leaders, and global practitioners onto one platform so that ideas move beyond prototypes toward production-grade solutions with real users, measurable impact, and international relevance.

| Strategic Goal: Position Bangladesh not merely as a user of artificial intelligence, but as a trusted source of AI-native builders, teams, and solutions for the global economy. |

**SECTION 02**

WHY THIS MATTERS NOW

The AI Shift Is Already Happening

The world is in the middle of the fastest technological transition since the internet. Artificial intelligence has moved from research labs into everyday economic infrastructure. Within just a few years, AI systems have become capable of writing software, generating multimedia content, analyzing massive datasets, and automating complex decision-making.

Global analysts estimate AI could contribute more than US\$15 trillion to the global economy by 2030, while companies rapidly restructure around AI-native product teams that combine developers, domain experts, and intelligent systems to build solutions faster than traditional methods. Multimodal models that understand text, images, audio, and video simultaneously are already transforming healthcare, finance, education, agriculture, and commerce. At the same time, misinformation, deepfakes, and data manipulation make trustworthy, sovereign AI increasingly critical for societies and institutions worldwide.

Why the Timing Matters for Bangladesh

Bangladesh has a population of over 170 million people, more than half under the age of 30, with millions studying in universities, colleges, polytechnics, and technical training programs. Every year hundreds of thousands of graduates enter a rapidly digitizing global economy. Meanwhile, thousands of Non-Resident Bangladeshi (NRB) professionals already work at leading global technology companies, startups, and research institutions.

The opportunity is clear: if Bangladesh can connect its young talent with global expertise and train them to build real AI systems — not just learn about them — it can become a major contributor to the global digital workforce. The challenge is that most local innovation still focuses on adopting technology rather than producing it. Countries that develop the ability to build and deploy AI solutions will define the next generation of economic leadership.

| The Congress creates a platform where Bangladeshi builders, NRB technologists, universities, and industry leaders work together to develop AI-native products that compete globally — catalyzing a national shift from AI adoption to AI production. |

**SECTION 07**

AI DEVELOPMENT WORKFLOW & REFERENCE ARCHITECTURE

This workflow guides teams in transforming ideas into deployable AI systems — not just prototypes. It mirrors how modern AI startups and enterprise teams actually build software.

Recommended Build Process for AI-Native Applications

| PHASE | ACTIVITIES | TOOLS / TECHNOLOGIES | OUTCOME |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Problem Definition | Define the user problem, audience, and measurable impact; identify why AI is essential | Research tools, LLM brainstorming (Claude / ChatGPT / Gemini) | Clear problem statement & solution concept |
| Architecture Design | Design input data, AI layers, storage, outputs; define agents and workflow | Cursor / Claude Code with markdown architecture files | Documented system blueprint |
| Prompt Engineering | Design structured prompts controlling reasoning, output format, and retrieval | Lovable, Cursor, Claude Code | Reliable, efficient AI responses |
| Knowledge Layer | Build contextual intelligence with RAG; store documents and embeddings | Vector DBs (Supabase / PGVector) or Graph DBs | Context-aware reasoning |
| AI Model Integration | Integrate LLMs for reasoning, generation, and interaction; add specialized agents | Claude / GPT / Gemini, MCP agent frameworks | Functional intelligence layer |
| Application Development | Build frontend, backend APIs, and workflow orchestration | Lovable edge functions, Supabase backend | Functional application |
| Agent Orchestration | Implement multi-agent collaboration for specialized tasks | MCP (Model Context Protocol), agentic frameworks | Automated reasoning pipelines |
| Testing & Validation | Evaluate performance, accuracy, bias, and usability; run edge cases | LLM evaluation tools, automated test pipelines | Stable, reliable system |
| Deployment Prep | Prepare scalable hosting with security, monitoring, cost optimization | Cloud-native (serverless / containerized) | Production-ready deployment |
| Impact & Scalability | Demonstrate global scale, API integration, enterprise workloads | APIs, modular architecture, cloud scaling | Enterprise-ready product |

AI-Native Reference Architecture

**SECTION 09**

COMPETITION 1 — TEAM-BUILT PRODUCT

The Team-Built Product competition is the flagship collaborative track. Teams of three to five members build a production-minded AI product within one chosen global domain over the six-month journey, then present it to judging panels on Congress Day. The competition evaluates AI-native systems — not ideas or interface mockups.

9.1 Submission Framework

Every team must submit a structured package explaining both the problem being solved and the system being built. This allows judges, mentors, and partners to understand how each solution works, why it matters, and how it could evolve into a real product.

| SUBMISSION COMPONENT | DESCRIPTION | WHY IT MATTERS |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Problem Definition | The problem, target users, and real-world need addressed | Establishes relevance and importance |
| AI Architecture | System architecture: input sources, models, pipelines, outputs | Demonstrates technical design and transparency |
| Data & Sovereignty Strategy | Data sources, preparation, privacy, governance, and ownership | Ensures responsible, compliant data practice |
| Demo (Live or Recorded) | Working demonstration in a real user scenario | Confirms the system functions as described |
| Scalability Roadmap | How the system scales beyond prototype — infra, deployment, roadmap | Encourages real-world implementation thinking |
| Ethical Safeguards | Bias mitigation, privacy protection, responsible-AI design | Aligns with global standards for trustworthy AI |

9.2 Preliminary Submission — “Vibe to Production in 180 Seconds”

The preliminary submission is the first proof of execution. It is not about presenting an idea — it is about demonstrating that the team can move from concept to a working AI system. Every team submits a 3-minute (180-second) structured video pitch by the preliminary deadline; this determines advancement to the Congress finals.

| TIME SEGMENT | WHAT YOU MUST SHOW | WHAT EVALUATORS LOOK FOR |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 0:00 – 0:30 — Problem | Define the problem, users, and urgency (local \+ global context) | Clarity, relevance |
| 0:30 – 1:00 — Solution | Your AI-driven solution and its differentiation | Simplicity, uniqueness |
| 1:00 – 2:00 — Demo / Flow | Early prototype, mock flow, or system walkthrough | Feasibility, logic |
| 2:00 – 2:30 — AI Approach | Models, RAG, data, personalization, tools | Depth, structure |
| 2:30 – 3:00 — Impact & Next Step | Value, potential users, and what comes next | Vision, potential |

Mandatory Requirements (Preliminary Stage)  
▶  A 3-minute structured video pitch

▶  Clear problem and user definition

▶  An AI-native approach (LLM / RAG / Graph / ML)

▶  Basic system flow (input → AI → output)

▶  An initial demo, prototype, or workflow (early-stage acceptable)

▶  Consideration for Bangla / localization

▶  Defined potential impact (KPIs or expected outcomes)

Common Mistakes to Avoid  
▶  Submitting only an idea with no system thinking

▶  No clear AI explanation

▶  Overcomplicating instead of focusing on one core use case

▶  No demo or workflow visualization

▶  Ignoring real users or real problems

| Final mindset: you are not judged on completion. You are judged on your ability to execute. Be clear, real, and buildable. |
| :---- |

9.3 Congress-Day Evaluation Format

Finalist teams are judged once, using a single, consistent evaluation. Because of the number of teams, the judging is split into two time-boxed group sessions — these are not two different rounds; each team is judged a single time in its assigned group. Every team follows the same per-team format.

| ELEMENT | DETAIL |
| :---- | :---- |
| Per-team format | 5 minutes per team — 1 minute transition \+ 3 minutes pitch \+ 1 minute scoring |
| Group Session 1 | 11:00 AM – 1:00 PM (120 minutes) — approximately 14 teams per panel |
| Group Session 2 | 2:00 – 3:00 PM (60 minutes) — approximately 7 teams per panel |
| Presentation flow | Structured demo: problem → solution → demo → AI → impact, followed by judge questions |
| Scoring | Independent scoring against the unified rubric (Section 11), submitted immediately |
| Evaluation areas | Innovation · AI Implementation · Technical Execution · User Experience · Business Viability · Impact & Scalability |

**SECTION 11**

OFFICIAL JUDGING FRAMEWORK (TEAM COMPETITION)

The Team-Built Product competition is evaluated through a unified, institutional-grade framework that measures innovation, technical depth, scalability, real-world impact, responsible AI, data sovereignty, commercial viability, and presentation clarity. Judges assess whether each solution is truly AI-native, architecturally sound, ethically responsible, and deployment-ready beyond a prototype. Projects are scored independently against the rubric — not comparatively against other teams. (The AI-Native Single Prompt Application Building Challenge is scored separately under its own three-category framework in Section 10.9.)

11.1 Judging Criteria & Weight Allocation (100 Points)

Each team is scored across five components, each worth 20 points, for a total of 100\. The eight Congress evaluation themes — innovation, technical excellence, business value, commercial viability, global scalability, real-world impact, responsible AI, and data sovereignty — are combined into these five components, with presentation assessed throughout.

| COMPONENT (20 PTS EACH) | WHAT JUDGES EVALUATE |
| :---- | :---- |
| Innovation & AI-Native Design | Originality, creativity, and non-obvious, transformative application of AI |
| Technical Excellence & Architecture | Architecture quality, AI integration depth, robustness, and explainability of the intelligence core |
| Business Value & Commercial Viability | Measurable economic value, clear value proposition, and a realistic path to a sustainable, monetizable business |
| Global Scalability & Real-World Impact | Capacity to scale to millions and international markets, plus jobs, productivity, exports, or societal benefit |
| Responsible AI, Data Sovereignty & Presentation | Fairness, explainability, bias mitigation, privacy, governance and data ownership — communicated with clarity and confidence |

| Total \= 5 × 20 \= 100 points. NRB / global collaboration serves as a qualitative advantage for global-standards alignment and is recognized within Technical Excellence & Architecture and Global Scalability & Real-World Impact. |
| :---- |

11.2 Score Bands & Interpretation

Each component is scored out of 20 using the defined bands below, ensuring fairness and consistency across all panels.

| SCORE BAND (OF 20\) | INTERPRETATION |
| :---- | :---- |
| 0 – 5 | Incremental, common, or weak — limited AI implementation or unclear value |
| 6 – 10 | Moderately differentiated — functional with limited depth or validation |
| 11 – 15 | Strong — well-structured AI-native design with clear relevance and outcomes |
| 16 – 20 | Breakthrough — globally competitive, production-grade, with documented safeguards |

| Final judging principle: reward solutions that combine AI-native engineering, measurable impact, ethical responsibility, data sovereignty, and global scalability — while encouraging cross-border collaboration. Judges' decisions are final. |

**SECTION 13**

ROLES, TEAM STRUCTURE & THE WINNING FORMULA

13.1 Stakeholder Roles

| ROLE | PRIMARY RESPONSIBILITIES | KEY PRINCIPLES |

| Judges | Evaluate each team using the official rubric across all criteria | Objectivity, consistency, no conflict of interest |
| Organizers | Provide platform, tools, logistics, and global ecosystem access (NRB, industry) | Neutrality, structure, operational excellence |

13.3 Team Composition (Prescriptive — Critical for Winning)

A strong team is not just technical. It must combine technical depth, business thinking, diversity, and global perspective.

| DIMENSION | HOW TO IMPLEMENT | WHY IT MATTERS |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Technical Capability | Ensure 2–3 members can build full-stack AI systems | Core execution capability |
| Business / Domain Expertise | Include 1 member focused on users, value, and adoption | Bridges idea → real-world usage |
| Women Participation | Encourage at least one female participant per team (bonus points) | Diversity improves innovation quality |
| Polytechnic / Madrasah Inclusion | Form cross-institution teams with applied backgrounds | Broader access and grounded thinking |
| NRB / Diaspora Participation | Assign an NRB/expert a role in architecture, strategy, or data | Global standards and competitiveness |

13.4 Top 10 Mistakes Teams Make (and How to Avoid Them)

| \# | COMMON MISTAKE | HOW TO AVOID IT |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 1 | Using AI as a gimmick (a chatbot with no real intelligence) | Start with the problem; design the AI core first |
| 2 | No clear problem definition | Define: who is the user, what pain, why now; add measurable KPIs |
| 3 | Weak architecture thinking (no input → AI → output flow) | Draw architecture early with Cursor/MD files; keep it modular |
| 4 | Ignoring real data (fake or hardcoded) | Use scrapers/APIs; build RAG \+ GraphDB for real context |
| 5 | No personalization (same output for all users) | Build user profiles; adapt to behavior/data |
| 6 | No Bangla / localization (English-only) | Add Bangla UI, voice, and low-bandwidth options |
| 7 | Overbuilding features, none complete | Focus on one core use case; make it work perfectly |
| 8 | No scalability thinking (works only on local demo) | Design APIs, cloud-ready, modular architecture |
| 9 | Poor demo & storytelling | Follow the structure: Problem → Solution → Demo → AI → Impact |
| 10 | Not leveraging team diversity | Include business/domain roles \+ NRB/global perspective |

**Bonus critical mistake:** Not using modern AI tools properly — coding everything manually loses the speed advantage. Use Lovable, Cursor, Claude Code, MCP, RAG, and agents to build faster and smarter.

13.5 The Winning Formula

Winning teams consistently:

▶  Start with a real problem

▶  Build a clear AI architecture

▶  Use real data \+ RAG \+ Graph reasoning

▶  Add personalization \+ Bangla localization

▶  Show a working demo with measurable impact

▶  Design for global scalability

| This moves a team from *“good student project”* to *“global AI product ready for deployment.”* |

**SECTION 15**

ETHICS, RESPONSIBLE AI & DATA SOVEREIGNTY
